Of all the hundreds of date varieties, none carries the standing of **ajwa** — the near-black, tender date grown in the orchards of Madinah. No date holds more meaning in the Islamic tradition, and none is more frequently imitated.

## What genuine ajwa is like

True ajwa is **small to medium, very dark — almost black — with fine wrinkles** and a soft, dense bite. The sweetness is restrained, closer to cocoa and dried fig than to sugar. It should never be hard, glossy-wet, or candy-sweet: those are signs of other varieties, or dates that have been treated to imitate ajwa's look.

## Where imitation happens

Because ajwa commands several times the price of ordinary dates, dark varieties from elsewhere are routinely sold under its name. The protections to look for:

- **"Saudi Made" certification on the box** — genuine ajwa is packed and sealed at origin, and reputable producers print their certification on the pack.
- **A sealed, branded box** — loose "ajwa" by the kilo from an unmarked crate deserves scepticism.
- **A supplier who names the producer.** We stock boxed ajwa from **Tamruk** and **Tahira**, both established Saudi producers, both certified — and we are happy to tell you exactly which is in stock.

## How ajwa is enjoyed

Traditionally an odd number at a time; to open the fast in Ramadan; alongside Arabic coffee or milk. The sealed gift boxes have made ajwa one of the most given presents for Ramadan, weddings, and returning Hajj and Umrah pilgrims.

## Storage

Keep boxes cool and dry; refrigerate after opening and the dates will hold for months. Bring to room temperature before serving — the texture rewards it.
