Walk into any traditional grocer from Marrakesh to Kuala Lumpur and you will find the same two jars side by side: **raw honey** and **black seed**. The pairing of the two — the blessed seed, *habbat al-barakah*, folded into honey — is among the oldest recorded preparations in the Islamic tradition.

## What black seed is

Black seed is the seed of *Nigella sativa*, a small flowering plant cultivated across the Middle East and South Asia for millennia. The seeds are tiny, matte black and intensely aromatic — peppery, slightly bitter, with a resinous edge. They appear whole in breads and pickles, pressed into oil, and — most classically — combined with honey.

## Why honey

Honey does two jobs in the pairing: it carries the seed's strong flavour gently, and it preserves. A well-made black seed honey balances the seed's pepper against the honey's sweetness so that neither dominates. The best versions use raw honey and genuine nigella sativa and nothing else — check the ingredients line; it should be two items long.

## The ways people take it

- **A spoonful in the morning** — by far the most common habit, taken straight.
- **Stirred into warm water or milk** — never boiling, which flattens the flavour of raw honey.
- **Over yoghurt or porridge** — where the gentle spice earns its place at breakfast.
